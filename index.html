How much of the new web is written by AI?

An independent, longitudinal dataset of AI-generated content in English-language news on the public web. Everything here is a population-level estimate with a confidence range, never a verdict on a single document. Data as of 2026-10-07 (UTC) · methodology v1.2.

AI-style vocabulary in English news peaked at 1.92× its pre-ChatGPT level in December 2023 and is now 0.68× its pre-ChatGPT level (September 2026, 95% range 0.57–0.80). A falling index does not by itself mean less AI writing: these words come from 2023–24 studies, and newer models use some of them less. The common-word list and the drift band help tell the two apart.

Since ChatGPT

How often two lists of words appear compared with English news before ChatGPT (1.00, dashed line). Science-study words (“delve”, “intricate”, “showcase”…) are the ones published studies found most over-used by ChatGPT; common words (“crucial”, “insight”, “significant”…) rose in the same studies but are also everyday news vocabulary. Circles: one week per month since 2022-12 (2,000 articles each, from the CC-NEWS archive). Squares: weeks collected as they happen (3,000 articles). Lines through each mark are 95% ranges. Week by week

Each row is one prospective week. Both columns are population-level estimates with 95% ranges. The index compares the rate of a documented list of words that rose after ChatGPT with the pre-2022-11 baseline; the floor counts documents containing unmistakable chatbot leftovers. The drift band (how much the index moved before ChatGPT) is not measured yet, so read small shifts with care.

Methodology and version

Methodology v1.2. Documents come from Common Crawl CC-NEWS, sampled at random and kept with the exact location of the raw record. Estimates are population-level only; we do not label individual documents. Read the method.
