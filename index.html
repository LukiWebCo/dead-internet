How much of the new web is written by AI?

An independent, longitudinal dataset of AI-generated content in English-language news on the public web. Everything here is a population-level estimate with a confidence range, never a verdict on a single document. Data as of 2026-10-07 (UTC) · methodology v1.2.

AI-style vocabulary in English news peaked at 1.74× its pre-ChatGPT level in December 2023 and is now 0.61× its pre-ChatGPT level (September 2026, 95% range 0.50–0.73). A falling index does not by itself mean less AI writing: these words come from 2023–24 studies, and newer models use some of them less. The common-word list and the drift band help tell the two apart.

Since ChatGPT

How often two lists of words appear compared with English news before ChatGPT (1.00, dashed line). Science-study words (“delve”, “intricate”, “showcase”…) are the ones published studies found most over-used by ChatGPT; common words (“crucial”, “insight”, “significant”…) rose in the same studies but are also everyday news vocabulary. Circles: one week per month since 2022-12 (2,000 articles each, from the CC-NEWS archive). Squares: weeks collected as they happen (3,000 articles). Lines through each mark are 95% ranges. Methodology and version

Methodology v1.2. Documents come from Common Crawl CC-NEWS, sampled at random and kept with the exact location of the raw record. Estimates are population-level only; we do not label individual documents. Read the method.
