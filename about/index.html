Method

The full details. For the short, plain-words version, see Explain it like I’m five.

What is measured

How much English-language news text published on the open web shows the fingerprints of AI writing, week by week, compared with news from before ChatGPT (before November 2022). The result is a trend for the whole population of sampled articles, with a 95% confidence range.

Where the documents come from

Common Crawl CC-NEWS, a public crawl of news sites. Each week we draw random pieces of its files, extract the article text, keep English documents with high language-detection confidence, and cap how many come from one site. The raw record location is stored with every document. The older weeks (back to 2019) are a smaller, separate “retrospective” sample, used only as the baseline and never mixed with the weekly data.

To show the years since ChatGPT, we also sample one week per month from December 2022 onwards (the week that holds the 15th), 2,000 documents each, from the same archive with the same method. This monthly series is shown separately from the weekly data, which has 3,000 documents a week and narrower ranges.

The two numbers

Word-shift index. Studies of scientific writing found words that became much more common after ChatGPT, such as “delve”, “underscore” and “intricate” (Kobak et al., Science Advances 2025; Liang et al., ICML 2024). We count a fixed list of them and divide by the rate in the pre-2022-11 baseline. 1.00 means no change; the range comes from resampling the documents.

Drift band. The 95% range only covers sampling error. News vocabulary also drifts with topics and outlets. To measure that, we score 2021 and early 2022 against 2019–20 as if they were new weeks; there was no ChatGPT then, so any shift is drift. The largest shift seen is the drift band, shown next to the index.

Held-out check. One document in five from before November 2022, chosen by a fixed hash of its record ID, is never used in the baseline. It is scored like a new week and must show no shift; the result is published with each methodology version.

Leftover-phrase floor. The share of documents containing text a person pasted without reading, such as “As an AI language model” or “Regenerate response”. It is a minimum. Most AI text has no such trace, so the true share is higher by an unknown amount. Such leftovers turn out to be very rare in news: a search of the first 8,835 post-ChatGPT articles for 18 candidate phrases found none, so the page pools every published week to give the tightest honest upper bound rather than a weekly figure that is always zero.

Beyond news

Each population has its own pre-ChatGPT baseline, hold-out and drift band, and is never pooled with another. Order chosen by the owner on 2026-10-08: forums first (Hacker News comments and text posts through its public search API: random 5-minute windows inside each week, at least 80 words, English, at most 3 texts per author a week; author names are never stored), and Stack Overflow answers (Stack Exchange API: random 30-minute windows, code removed; the site banned AI-written answers in December 2022 and its volume fell, so that population itself changed); then scholarly papers: arXiv abstracts by week of first submission, leaving out of the baseline any paper revised on or after 2022-11-01 (arXiv serves the latest abstract; this keeps post-ChatGPT text out of the baseline, but it also favours papers that were never revised), with PubMed next; then the wider web from Common Crawl, by kind of site.

What is not measured

  • No per-document verdicts. Detectors that label one article as “AI” or “human” make errors that fall on real writers, especially non-native English writers. Estimates for a whole population avoid that, so we publish none for single articles or sites.
  • The word list comes from science writing. Whether it shifts the same way in news is part of what is being measured, and models change their habits over time. A rising index means more of the shift, not a count of AI articles.
  • Other languages, and sites that are not news, are out of scope for this version.
  • Text that was edited by a person, or written by a model with a different style, is mostly invisible to both numbers.

Versions

Methodology version in use: v1.2. Changes to sampling, language handling, text extraction, word lists or phrases each get a new version, and old documents can be re-analysed under a new one. Limits: text extraction is a simple heuristic, and CC-NEWS covers news-type sites, not the whole web.
